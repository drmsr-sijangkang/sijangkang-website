# Welcome to Sijangkang

*A hidden gem in Selangor where mangrove forests, river life, and Javanese-Malay heritage meet.*

---

## About Sijangkang

Tucked within the Telok Panglima Garang area of the Kuala Langat District in Selangor, Kampung Sijangkang is a quiet riverside village bordering the Langat River — about 12 km from Klang. What was once an overlooked village has, through the sheer determination of its residents, become one of Selangor's most inspiring community-led ecotourism stories: a former illegal dumping ground transformed into a thriving mangrove recreational park.

Visitors come here for a slower pace of life — boardwalks through mangrove forest, river views, traditional Malay architecture, and the warmth of a community that built its main attraction with its own hands.

**Quick Facts**
- **State:** Selangor
- **District:** Kuala Langat (Telok Panglima Garang)
- **Postcode:** 42500
- **Time Zone:** UTC+8 (Malaysia Standard Time)
- **Administered by:** Kuala Langat Municipal Council (Majlis Perbandaran Kuala Langat)
- **Distance from Klang:** ~12 km
- **Bordering:** Langat River

## The Story Behind the Name

The name *Sijangkang* is a blend of two things close to the village's heart: the river that runs beside it, and the **jangkang tree** (*Xylopia ferruginea*) — a wild forest tree, similar to the rubber tree, whose seed pods are known to burst open to scatter their seeds.

The village's roots go back to around 1892, when Javanese settlers from Kampung Pandamaran in Klang explored the area and eventually made it their home along the banks of the Langat River. In Malay, the prefix "Si" often carries a personal, affectionate tone — similar to "si Kecil" or "si Comel" — giving Sijangkang's name the warmth of a story passed down through generations rather than just a place marker on a map.

## Getting There

Sijangkang sits in the Telok Panglima Garang area of Kuala Langat District, Selangor, bordering Klang.

- **Coordinates:** 2°56′36″N, 101°27′24″E
- **Nearest towns:** Telok Panglima Garang and Klang (Sijangkang borders both)
- **By car:** The easiest route is via the **Shah Alam Expressway (KESAS)** — take the exit heading toward **Banting**, then follow local signage into Kampung Sijangkang. The road narrows as you enter the village, passing houses, food stalls, and an old Javanese cemetery (opened in 1890) along the way.
- **Landmark address (Mangrove Park):** Jalan Batu 8, Kampung Sijangkang, 42500 Telok Panglima Garang, Kuala Langat, Selangor
- **Nearby attractions worth combining into a trip:** Pasaraya Borong GM Klang (wholesale market), Bukit Jugra, and Pantai Morib
- **Parking:** Limited parking is available near the Mangrove Recreational Park (space for a handful of buses/cars); roadside parking is also common near the entrance.

*Note: The village road is a single narrow lane — buses and lorries can pass, but it's best to drive carefully and allow extra time.*

## What Makes Sijangkang Special

### 🌿 Sijangkang Mangrove Recreational Park (Taman Rekreasi Paya Bakau Kg Sijangkang)
The village's proudest achievement. What was once an illegal dumping site along a 6 km stretch of riverbank was reclaimed by local volunteers starting in 2015 and turned into a 24-hectare mangrove ecotourism destination — now featuring an 800-metre wooden boardwalk (built using timber donated by villagers), guided nature trails, and educational signage identifying local mangrove flora and fauna along the way. The park has since won recognition including the "Friends of River" award from Selangor's water authority (LUAS) and international acknowledgment from JAIF and IGES.
- **Entrance fee:** RM2 (adults), RM1 (children under 12)
- **Good for:** birdwatching, photography, nature walks, learning about mangrove conservation, school/corporate community programs
- **Tip:** Bring insect repellent and water — the boardwalk is shaded but mosquitoes are common, and long sleeves are recommended.

### 🏠 A 70-Year-Old Traditional Malay House
Sijangkang is also home to a beautifully preserved traditional Malay house, originally built in Teluk Intan, Perak, then carefully dismantled and rebuilt in Sijangkang using its original cengal and resak timber. The house features over 100 different traditional carving designs and has been used as a filming location for local dramas and productions — a rare, hands-on glimpse into pre-modern Malay architecture and craftsmanship.

### 🍽️ Local Flavours
Visitors can enjoy affordable, home-style Malay cuisine around the village, including fluffy roti canai, hearty lontong served with fried chicken and sambal sotong, and Malacca-style asam pedas cooked in claypots with fresh fish such as stingray.

## Community & Village Life

Sijangkang's biggest attraction wasn't built by a developer — it was built by its own residents.

- **Community-led transformation:** In August 2015, local resident Mohd Suhaimi Sanusi rallied just 15 volunteers to begin cleaning up the dumping site through monthly gotong-royong (communal work) sessions. With support from the Kuala Langat Municipal Council, corporations, and universities, the effort grew into what is now the Sijangkang Mangrove Recreational Park — entirely self-funded and still run today by volunteer committee members ranging from their 30s to 75 years old.
- **Local governance:** The village is overseen by the Jawatankuasa Pembangunan dan Keselamatan Kampung (JPKK), the village development and security committee, led by a Ketua Kampung (village headman) who coordinates community welfare, security, and development.
- **Ongoing volunteering:** The park continues to host regular gotong-royong sessions, mangrove tree-planting programs, and educational visits from schools, universities, and corporate CSR groups (past participants have included Top Glove Foundation and Muhibbah Engineering, among others).
- **Festive traditions:** Like much of Malay village life, Sijangkang comes alive during Hari Raya Aidilfitri and Aidiladha, with open houses welcoming neighbours and visitors alike to share meals and celebrate together.
- **Visiting respectfully:** As this is a residential kampung, visitors are encouraged to be mindful while driving through, greet locals warmly, and consider joining a gotong-royong or tree-planting session if visiting in a group — the community welcomes those who want to contribute, not just observe.

## Plan Your Visit

Weekends tend to be the liveliest time to visit, when local families and day-trippers head to the mangrove park. Mornings are best for cooler weather, better birdwatching, and softer light for photography. Whether you're coming for a quiet nature walk, a taste of traditional Malay architecture, or simply to see what a community can build together, Sijangkang offers a side of Selangor that's easy to miss — and hard to forget once you've seen it.
